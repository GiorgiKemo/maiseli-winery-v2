Our Story

Four generations, one qvevri

The story of Maiseli Winery began in 1908, when our great-grandfather buried the family’s first qvevri in the earth and began making wine using the ancient traditional Georgian method.

More than a century has passed since then, yet our family’s winemaking tradition has never been interrupted. The knowledge, experience, and deep respect for wine have been passed down from generation to generation.

Even today, we continue to make wine in the very same qvevri that was placed in the earth in 1908 — a vessel that has witnessed generations of our family and more than a century of winemaking history.

Our Vineyard

With respect for the land, work by hand, and harmony with nature.

Maiseli Winery owns a 2-hectare vineyard, where we grow indigenous Georgian grape varieties — Kisi, Rkatsiteli, Tsolikouri, and Saperavi.

In caring for our vineyard, we place particular emphasis on maintaining the natural balance of the ecosystem, preserving soil health, and supporting the healthy development of the vines. We use only products permitted in organic farming, protecting the vineyard’s natural environment while producing healthy, high-quality grapes.

Most of the work is done by hand — from tending and pruning the vines to carefully selecting and harvesting the grapes. This hands-on approach lets us give individual attention to each vine.

Our Winemaking

Three directions, one soul

I

The foundation

Traditional Qvevri Wines

We make our wines using the traditional method in qvevri buried in the earth, where the wine naturally develops and matures. Our goal is to preserve in each wine the natural character of the grape, the distinctive qualities of its origin, and the authenticity of Georgian qvevri winemaking.

For Maiseli, the qvevri is more than a vessel — it is part of our family history.

  • Saperavi Qvevri
  • Kisi Qvevri
  • Rkatsiteli Qvevri
  • Tsolikouri Qvevri
II

A distinctive direction

Our Heritage — Qvevri & French Oak

Saperavi and Rkatsiteli, made in qvevri using the traditional method, are further aged in French oak barrels. The qvevri preserves the wine’s Georgian identity and authentic character, while French oak adds greater depth, structure, and complexity.

A meeting of heritage and contemporary vision — with Maiseli’s own signature.

  • Saperavi Qvevri · French Oak
  • Rkatsiteli Qvevri · French Oak
III

A Georgian tradition

Naturally Semi-Sweet Wines

Kindzmarauli and Tvishi — two distinctive Georgian wines in which natural sweetness, varietal aromas, and refined balance come together harmoniously. We preserve their natural character and individuality, reflecting both their origin and Georgia’s unique winemaking culture.

  • Kindzmarauli · Semi-Sweet Red
  • Tvishi · Semi-Sweet White

Homeland of Wine

8,000 vintages of Georgia

For at least 8000 years, the vine has carried symbolic and sacred meaning for the Georgian people. For Georgians, winemaking and wine drinking has always been a form of art.

Scroll through time
c. 6000 BC

The first wine

Earthenware jars with residual wine compounds, found at two sites south of Tbilisi, revealed the earliest evidence of grape winemaking — some 8,000 years old.

V–VI millennium BC

The oldest qvevri

Georgia’s National Museum in Tbilisi keeps a unique qvevri dated as far back as the 5th–6th millennium.

VIII–VII c. BC

Homer’s Odyssey

Georgian wine is mentioned in the Odyssey — and many details of Georgian winemaking have remained unchanged since Homer’s time.
